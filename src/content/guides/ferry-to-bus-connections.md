Ferries in the Cyclades run to a published timetable. Buses run to a published
timetable. Neither operator coordinates with the other, and no arrangement
guarantees that a bus will wait. That single fact is the reason the ferry-to-bus
connection is the most stressful five minutes of an island trip, and it is why
almost nobody writes the answer down.

This page is the answer, per island, as of **26 September 2026**.

## The short answer

**There is no connection to rely on.** A late ferry does not delay a bus. A bus
that has left will not return. On every island we cover, the bus timetables are
built around residents' commuting hours and the school day, not around ferry
arrivals — because the operators are not in the business of meeting boats.

What this means in practice is that the last departure of the day is the number
you need, and you need it *before* you sail, not after you land.

## Port to hub, per island

| Island | Main port | Bus hub | Buses from the port after a mid-afternoon landing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Naxos | Naxos Port (Gialos) | Naxos Town (Chora), 5 min walk or 1 bus | Frequent through the day; five lines converge on Chora |
| Paros | Parikia | Parikia, 2 min walk | Parikia is the hub, so a late landing loses less than elsewhere |
| Santorini | Athinios (main) | Fira, 20 min by road | One line to Fira, then the Fira network; Athinios has very little |
| Milos | Adamas | Adamas, 5 min walk | Adamas is the hub, so a late landing loses less than elsewhere |
| Ios | Gialos (Port) | Chora, 15 min walk or the one line | One line, and a short day |

Two of these are forgiving because the port *is* the hub. That is the single most
useful thing to know when choosing an island for a short stay with a late
arrival: **Paros and Milos are the forgiving ones, because on both the port and
the bus station are the same place.**

Santorini is the difficult case, and not because of the buses. Athinios is the
busy port, and the road up to Fira is the single most congested stretch on the
island. A landing at 19:00 can mean arriving in Fira at 19:40.

## Why the timetables do not line up, and never will

Three reasons, none of them anybody's fault:

1. **The buses are for residents.** They are timed to the school day, to shift
   changes, to the market. A ferry arrives whenever the sea allows.
2. **The operators do not coordinate.** We found no published arrangement
   between any Cyclades ferry company and any bus operator guaranteeing a wait.
3. **The vehicle is shared.** The same bus does a school run in the morning and
   a ferry run in the afternoon. Holding it for a late boat costs a child's
   journey home.

Where a harbour is small enough that the driver can see the ferry coming, an
informal wait sometimes happens. It is not something to plan a day around.

## What to do about it

**Before you sail**, check the last departure from your port on your island. Each
planner shows it, and every line page states the first and last service of the
day. If your ferry lands after it, you need a plan:

- **Stay in the port town that night.** On Paros, Milos, Naxos and Ios this is
  cheap and easy: the port has tavernas and the walk into town is short. On
  Santorini, stay in Athinios or Fira and go up the caldera in the morning.
- **Budget for a taxi.** Island taxis are metered or pre-agreed, they wait for
  the last ferry, and they are the reason the last-ferry problem is survivable
  rather than fatal. This is the one category of transport on these islands we
  do not publish times for, and the reason is that there is nothing published to
  read.
- **Assume the bus will not wait, and plan as if it will not.** Every plan that
  works if the bus sometimes waits also works if it never does.

## What we cannot tell you

We do not publish live vehicle positions, because no Cyclades bus operator
publishes one and we will not go looking for a way around that. Every departure
time on this site is the operator's own printed schedule, labelled with the date
we retrieved it. Where an intermediate stop time is interpolated rather than
printed, we label it as an estimate.

The ferry timetable itself is the operator's, not ours, and it changes. Check it
before you sail, and treat the arrival time as approximate.

## Questions this page answers

**How long should I allow between a ferry arriving and the next bus?** On every
island we cover, the operators publish bus timetables on a clock schedule and
ferry timetables on their own, with no coordination between the two. The honest
answer is that there is no reliable connection and never has been: a ferry that
is late does not cause a bus to wait, and a bus that has left does not come back.
Plan on walking, or on a taxi, if your ferry lands after the last bus of the day.

**Is there ever a bus that waits for a late ferry?** No. We found no published
arrangement between any Cyclades ferry operator and any bus operator guaranteeing
a wait. Where a harbour is small enough that the driver can see the ferry coming,
an informal wait sometimes happens, but it is not something to plan a day around.

**What time does the last bus leave each port?** It depends entirely on the
island and the line, and it is the single most important number to check before
you book a ferry. Each island planner shows the first and last departure for
every line. Data verified 26 September 2026.

**Which island has the most generous ferry-to-bus connections?** Naxos, by a
distance: it has the largest network and the most departures spread across the
day, so a late landing is more likely to find a bus still to come. Ios is the
opposite case — one line, three stops, and a short day.

## The honest summary

The ferry-to-bus connection in the Cyclades is unmanaged, and the reason is
structural rather than a failing. There is nothing to wait for and nothing to
optimise. The only lever you have is the last-bus time, and the only way to use
it is to check it before you commit to a sailing.
