"Is the bus good on this island?" is the question everyone asks and nobody
answers with numbers, because the answer depends entirely on where you are
staying. Here are the measured figures for the five islands we cover, taken from
the operator timetables on **26 September 2026**.

## The numbers

| Island | Lines | Stops | Journeys published | Operator |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Naxos | 5 | 55 | 112 | KTEL Naxos |
| Paros | 14 | 25 | 84 | KTEL Paros |
| Santorini | 10 | 20 | 62 | KTEL Santorini |
| Milos | 7 | 19 | 52 | Milos Buses |
| Ios | 1 | 3 | 6 | Ios Local Buses |

Read the line-count column on its own and you will get the wrong answer, which
is why the rest of this page exists.

## Line count is not usefulness

**Paros has nearly three times as many lines as Naxos.** On a map that looks
like the better bus island. It is not, for a visitor, and the reason is that
Paros's fourteen lines are mostly short local services — Naoussa to Alyko,
Naoussa to Kolymbithres, and the several small services around the northern
villages. They are frequent and they are useful, but they do not take you
anywhere far.

**Naxos's five lines are trunk routes.** They radiate from the port and Chora
out to Apollon in the south, to the mountain villages in the interior, to
Apeiranthos in the north-east, to the west coast. Five lines, and between them
they cover the whole island. The published journey count reflects that: 112
plannable origin–destination pairs against Paros's 84, on a network that covers
a larger area.

So: **the most useful network is Naxos's; the most lines is Paros's.** If you
are staying in Naoussa and going to Antiparos, Paros wins. If you are staying
in Chora and want to reach Apeiranthos, only Naxos has that at all.

## Where the buses actually go

**Naxos** — the only island where a single day's bus trip can cross from the
south coast to the mountains. Chora and the port are the hub; Apollon, Agios
Georgios, Potamia, Filoti and Apeiranthos are all reachable by bus.

**Paros** — hub at Parikia, with a secondary cluster at Naoussa. Alyko,
Antiparos, Lefkes and the north are covered. The two ports, Parikia and Naoussa,
are close together, which makes Paros forgiving if your ferry lands late.

**Santorini** — ten lines, and they cover the places most people actually stay:
Fira, Kamari, Perissa, Imerovigli, Oia by bus-then-walk, plus the three ferry
terminals. The caldera rim itself has no bus, which is a deliberate
preservation decision by the island, not a gap in the network.

**Milos** — a private operator, seven lines from Adamas. Plaka, Pollonia,
Provatas, Sarakiniko and the airport. Small network, but the distances are
short and Adamas is both the port and the hub.

**Ios** — one line, three stops, six journeys. The Port–Chora–Mylopotas service.
This is genuinely all Ios has. It is not a data gap on our side; it is what the
island's operator runs. We would rather show you three honest stops than a
network we invented.

## So which is best?

It depends on three things, in this order:

1. **Where you are staying.** A network that does not reach your village is not
   a network, however frequent it is.
2. **How late you are out.** The last departure decides whether you need a taxi
   budget. On Naxos and Paros the day is long enough that most days end with an
   option. On Ios, a late afternoon is a taxi.
3. **How far you want to go without a car.** Naxos and Milos reward it.
   Santorini rewards it for the beaches and the villages, not for the caldera.

**The short version:** Naxos for range, Paros for density around Naoussa,
Santorini for the places people stay, Milos for short hops, Ios for walking.

## Questions this page answers

**Which Cyclades island has the most bus lines?** Paros, with fourteen, against
Naxos with five. But line count is a poor measure of usefulness: Paros's
fourteen lines are mostly short local services around Naoussa and Alyko, while
Naxos's five are long trunk routes that reach the whole island. Naxos has the
most useful network; Paros has the most lines.

**Which Cyclades island is hardest to get around without a car?** Santorini, for
a specific reason rather than a general one: Fira is effectively car-free and
walk-only, and the caldera rim has no bus at all. Everywhere else, a car-free day
is possible with planning. On Santorini, plan for one car day per stay.

**Is a bigger bus network always better for a visitor?** No. What matters is
whether the bus reaches the place you are staying, and how late the last one
runs. Santorini's ten lines cover Fira, Kamari, Perissa and the ferries, so it is
a good network for the places most people stay. Ios has one line and three
stops, which is poor, but the whole island is small enough to walk across in an
hour.

## What these numbers do not tell you

They do not tell you how often a bus actually comes, because we do not publish
frequency as a separate figure — the timetable shows it, and the planner shows
it. They do not tell you whether a bus is running, because there is no live feed
and we will not pretend otherwise. And they do not tell you which lines run in
winter, which is a different timetable entirely.

Every count above is read from the operator's own published schedule. If an
operator changes a line, our figures change with it, and the changelog records
when.
